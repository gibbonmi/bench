# Who starts a queued grade, and when is a claim live?

Blocked by: #6, #7
Type: Grill

### Question

ADR 0030 requires one lifetime owner, a normal-exit policy, and a census row for each new caller.
Which process starts a background grade and each queued grade?
When does a claim count as live, and what does the outcome `aborted` mean?

### Answer

A chain of grade processes runs the queue, and a claim is live while its owner lives in the current boot.
The reviewer chose this on 2026-10-27, after the map review.

- A lane pass that finds no live running grade starts the oldest queued grade, which can be its own.
- The grade starts as a detached session through the lifetime owner. It leaves the process group of the verb and of the harness, so it outlives `bench commit`.
- The lifetime owner gets a census row for this new caller under ADR 0030.
- When a grade ends, its process starts the oldest queued grade and then exits. No resident process exists.
- A claim names its owner pid and the boot id.
- A consumer treats a claim as ended when its owner is not alive, or when its boot id differs from the current boot.
- The outcome `aborted` names a grade that ended with no green and no red. A timeout, an interrupt, an infrastructure refusal, or a dead owner each gives `aborted`.

So a dead chain resumes at the next lane pass.
