Write all prose in ASD-STE100 Simplified Technical English.

## Writing rules

- Use the active voice. Name the agent: "Run the gate", not "The gate should be run".
- Use one topic per paragraph. Keep a paragraph to six sentences or fewer.
- Keep a procedural sentence to 20 words or fewer. Keep a descriptive sentence to 25 words or fewer.
- Give one instruction per sentence. Write an instruction in the imperative: "Open the file."
- Put the condition before the instruction: "If the gate is red, stop."
- Use the present tense for descriptions. Use the past tense only for an event that occurred before.
- Write the article ("the", "a") and the demonstrative ("this", "that"). Do not use telegraphic style.
- Do not use a gerund as a noun. Write "Before you build", not "Before building".
- Do not use a noun as a verb ("to gate", "to diff"). Use the approved verb: "run the gate", "compare the files".
- Use one word for one thing. Do not use a synonym for variety.
- Use the approved meaning of a word. "Follow" means "come after", not "obey" (use "obey"). "Check" is not a verb (use "make sure", "examine", "do a check").
- Do not make a noun cluster of more than three nouns. Rewrite it with a preposition.
- Use a warning or caution before the step it applies to, not after.
- Use a vertical list for three or more parallel items. Start each item with the same part of speech.

## Scope

- These rules apply to your prose, your commentary on tool calls, your summaries, and your questions.
- These rules do not apply to code, file paths, identifiers, commit messages, quoted output, or text that a project's own template fixes.
- A project instruction file (CLAUDE.md, AGENTS.md) owns structure: headings, labels, and exit sections. Obey it. Apply these rules to the sentences inside that structure.
- When an approved word is not available for a technical term, keep the technical name. Do not invent a paraphrase.
