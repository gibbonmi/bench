# How does a background red reach the author, and what does a consumer do with it?

Blocked by: #6
Type: Grill

### Question

The evidence store holds greens only, and a red authorizes nothing.
Where does the author see a background red or an aborted grade?
Does a consumer that finds a background red of its own key refuse at once, or run its own gate?

### Answer

A background outcome is a signal to the author, and it gives no consumer a refusal.
The reviewer chose this on 2026-10-27.

- Each background grade writes its outcome to the background record of its worktree. The outcome is green, red, aborted, or stopped, with the grade key, the tip, and the log path.
- Ticket 16 defines `aborted`, and ticket 13 defines `stopped`.
- The background record is its own record class. It is not the gate cache, and no consumer reads it as a verdict.
- `bench status` shows the latest background outcome of each worktree.
- The next `bench commit` in that worktree prints that outcome and its log path.
- A consumer that finds a background red of its own key runs its own gate. A background red never makes a consumer refuse.
- A background red writes no reader hold, as ticket 6 states.

The reason: a run at the lowest priority beside foreground load is more prone to the load-coupled reds that `roadmap/FT255.md` records.
