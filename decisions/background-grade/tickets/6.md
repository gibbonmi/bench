# How does a consumer meet an in-flight background grade?

Blocked by: #1
Type: Grill

### Question

Today a lookup refuses while a live holder keeps the checkout lock or a pending cache.
Where does a background grade keep its state?
Does a consumer of the same grade key wait for it before it refuses or runs?

### Answer

A background grade claims its grade key, and a consumer of that key waits for it.
The reviewer chose this on 2026-10-27, and sharpened it after the map review.

Writes:

- A background grade writes four places only:
  - the shared evidence store
  - the reader table, after a whole green
  - the delta record of its worktree
  - the background record of its worktree
- A background grade never writes a reader hold and never writes the gate cache of a checkout.
- A background grade takes no checkout lock.
- Ticket 14 places the delta green, and ticket 9 places the background record.

Claim:

- A background grade holds a claim on its grade key while it runs.
- The claim names the key, the worktree, the owner pid, and the boot id. Ticket 16 states when a claim is live.

Wait:

- A consumer that finds no green looks for a live claim on its own key. If it finds one, it waits until the claim ends.
- When the wait starts, the waited grade moves from the lowest priority to normal priority.
- The wait counts against the consumer's own gate timeout.
- A checkpoint waits before it takes the checkout lock.
- A landing waits before it takes the destination lease. Under the lease, it computes its key again and looks it up.
- After the wait, the consumer looks up its key again. A green answers it. Otherwise, the consumer runs its own gate.
- A consumer does not wait for a queued grade. A queued grade of the consumer's key leaves the queue when the consumer starts its own run.

Stop:

- Before a consumer runs its own gate, it stops the running grade and drops the queued grade of its worktree that hold another key.
- The consumer then waits until the stopped grade has exited, because both can write the delta record of the worktree.

Two foreground runs in one checkout keep today's refusal: the run that cannot take the checkout lock refuses and names the owner.
So the GR44 edge stays only between two foreground runs.
This closes the second reviewer question of `roadmap/FT451.md` for background grades.
