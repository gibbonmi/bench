# What subject does a background grade compute?

Blocked by: #3, #5
Type: Grill

### Question

For each consumer in scope, which tree, schedule, and running executable does the background grade reproduce?
Which inputs may the subject read while the author keeps editing the worktree?

### Answer

A background grade computes the exact run of the next consumer of its worktree.
The reviewer chose this on 2026-10-27, and sharpened it after the map review.

Chunk subject:

- A kit worktree with an open spec build grades the chunk subject. That subject is the HEAD tree of the lane-passed tip without the review record of the spec.
- The chunk grade takes the chunk id from the latest chunk entry of the review record at the tip. When no entry resolves, it runs the whole schedule.
- With that chunk id, the chunk grade takes the run decision of the chunk checkpoint. So a test-only change after a whole green runs the delta set.
- The chunk grade grades the subject alone. It runs no evidence pass and no record prose grade, because the checkpoint runs both on every call.
- The chunk grade runs as the executable that the wrapper resolves for the target worktree.
- A change of that executable before the checkpoint moves the grade key, so the checkpoint then runs its own gate.

Landing subject:

- Every other kit worktree grades the landing subject. That subject is the tree that the landing would publish from the tip, composed on the destination at the start of the grade.
- A spec-less landing applies no publish transform, so its subject is the tip composed on the destination.
- The landing grade runs as the authenticated broker process and takes the schedule of the destination, as the complete checkpoint does.

Both subjects:

- Every subject member reads the graded commit, the destination commit, or machine inputs. No member reads the live worktree.
- So an author edit, a plan commit, or a `bench probe` mutant during the run leaves the subject unchanged.
- A wrong derivation of the chunk id, the spec, or the landing deliverable only costs a lookup miss. A grade never writes a green under a key that it did not run.
