**FT243 (MEDIUM) — a capture entry the parser cannot see is reported as zero, not as a failure.**
`capture/learnings.md` has one grammar: a `## <date> — <title> [open]` heading.
A dated entry written as a bullet instead of a heading is skipped silently. It
produces no entry, no malformed record, and no parse failure, while the source
still reports as `parsed` with its full byte count. Both of the journal's
entries were invisible this way, and every surface agreed: `bench status` said
zero open learnings, and `bench roadmap --context` returned `learnings[0]`
beside `capture/learnings.md,parsed,1886`.

The consequence is a silent drop, not an inconvenience. The drain reads the
index as its complete inventory, so a drain driven by that inventory alone
discards every entry the writer got wrong — and the writer has no signal,
because the file it appended to looked exactly like the file it read.

The property: a capture source reports what it could not interpret. A dated line
that is not a well-formed entry becomes a malformed record the drain must
verdict, or the source reports a parse failure. Bytes parsed with zero entries
recovered and no diagnostic is the state that may not exist. The `[open]`
requirement already produces a malformed record when a heading lacks it; the gap
is everything that never reaches heading shape.

Occurrence: 2026-08-21 drain — both `capture/learnings.md` entries were invisible to `bench status` and to `bench roadmap --context`, which reported the file parsed at 1886 bytes with zero entries and no parse failure.
Occurrence: 2026-08-21 drain — the face repeated one drain later: two bullet-shaped entries written after this row was opened were again reported as `learnings[0]` beside `capture/learnings.md,parsed,1823`, with `parse_failures[0]` and `bench status` at zero open learnings.
