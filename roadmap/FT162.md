**FT162 (MEDIUM, decision required) — the handoff's pins resolve, and its shape has one owner.**
The session handoff is written by hand, and hand-copied pins do not resolve: the
file has carried a `HEAD` and a gate commit that are not commits in this
repository at all. Pins are generated from the tree or the file states that it
cannot pin, rather than carrying an abbreviation nobody checked.

The handoff must also carry the landing request token, or say plainly that
reauthorization is required — a landing that cannot be resumed because its token
died with the session is the failure this row exists for. When `bench handoff`
runs inside a worktree it records worktree-relative facts rather than the
destination's.

The handoff's timing is undecided too, and it collides with retained-source
review. The phase boundary requires writing the handoff, and committing it to
the destination is required because the landing's reconcile hard-resets an
uncommitted file — but that commit puts the destination ahead of the reviewed
source's frozen base, so the next `bench preflight review` reports `base-current`
red. The landing is unaffected by design, which is the problem: a preflight row
that is red, expected, and harmless trains sessions to read past red. Either the
handoff commit waits until after the landing, or `base-current` distinguishes a
source that is behind its destination from a source that is stale.

`bench handoff` also overwrites content it does not own: it replaces the file's
Next-command section with the board's leading signal, which erases the
phase-resume invocation the handoff contract asks for mid-build.

The other undecided part is the shape: one pruned `capture/session-handoff.md`, a
per-spec handoff, or a generated projection. Pick one — a second convention
alongside the first is the outcome to avoid.
